The shape of the trip

Ten days along the top; the nine nights sit in the gaps between them. Each night label straddles the two days it joins, so you can read where you go to bed and where you wake up. Five bases — Porto, one night in the Douro, one in Coimbra, one in Batalha, then Lisbon for the rest. The middle of the trip is three single nights in a row, which is the price of covering this much ground: the Douro on Wednesday, Coimbra and Tomar on Thursday, and two monasteries, Nazaré and Óbidos on the way into Lisbon on Friday.

Tue6

Paiva boardwalks and into the Douro

Arouca → Pinhão · ends in night 3 · The Vintage House
▶
08:30Breakfast, then check out of NOLIA with the bags
09:00Get out to Boavista — the desk is Avenida da Boavista 1203, about 3 km west of the centre, not walkable. Metro to Casa da Música then ~12 min on foot, or a 10-min Uber (~€7). With bags, take the Uber
09:30Collect the car — Porto Boavista Booked Inspect and photograph it, confirm the Via Verde transponder is fitted, and check the fuel policy is full-to-full
10:00Away — VCI south then the A32, ~1h05. Boavista is actually the better side of town for this: you're on the ring road in five minutes
11:00Passadiços do Paiva — 8.7 km of boardwalk along the Paiva gorge. Book the 11:00 timed slot, not 10:00 — the later pickup moved this
14:00Ponte 516 Arouca — the suspension bridge, usually a combined ticket
14:45Late lunch in Arouca — keep it to an hour
15:50Drive to Pinhão, ~1h50 — the last stretch is the N222 Régua → Pinhão, so you get the best road in the country in late-afternoon light
17:45Check in at The Vintage House, Pinhão. Riverside lawn and pool, and about an hour to sit by the water before dinner
20:00Dinner at the Rabelo, in the hotel — Booked Nothing to arrange and nowhere to drive after a long day (ask for the non-alcoholic pairing on the night)
This day is new. It replaces the Peneda-Gerês night and the PR24 Sistelo hike from the earlier draft, which the night structure no longer has room for. Paiva is the substitute: comparable effort, it sits directly on the Porto→Douro line rather than 2h45 north of it, and the gorge is arguably the more dramatic walk. Book the timed slot — it sells out.
Effort check. Paiva is 8.7 km with a lot of stepped descent and ascent. Allow three hours and don't schedule anything tight afterwards.
The 09:30 pickup costs you the pool afternoon. The day was built on an 08:30 collection; the booked slot is an hour later and at Boavista rather than in the centre, so everything shifts and check-in lands at 17:45 instead of 17:00. Dinner at 20:00 is still comfortable, but the unhurried afternoon is now about an hour. If you want it back, the lever is Ponte 516 — skip the bridge and you're in Pinhão by 17:00 as originally planned. Worth knowing before you book the Paiva slot: book 11:00.
Wed7

The Douro on foot in the morning, the N222 in the afternoon

Pinhão → Coimbra · ends in night 4 · Coimbra
▶
07:45Walk up to Casal de Loivos — ~45–60 min and about 250 m of climb straight out of Pinhão, to what a lot of people call the best view in the Douro. Best light of the day, and this is the moderate hike the itinerary lost with Sistelo
09:45Back down; breakfast at the hotel
10:45Check out of The Vintage House, bags in the car
11:00One-hour rabelo cruise from the Pinhão quay — two minutes' walk. Book the day before at the quay or online; departures are roughly hourly
12:15Pinhão station azulejo panels and the riverfront — twenty minutes, and it's on the way back to the car
12:45Drive the N222 west to Folgosa, ~25 min. You did this road inbound in evening light; you get it again from the other side
13:15Lunch at DOC, Rui Paula's glass room on the water at Folgosa — request the non-alcoholic pairing. Allow two hours and take the terrace (book)
15:20On west; Miradouro de São Leonardo de Galafura above Régua, a 20-min detour — the view that explains the whole valley, and your goodbye to it
15:50South on the A24 towards Viseu, ~55 min. The road climbs out of the gorge and the terraces stop abruptly — that's the real goodbye to the Douro, not the viewpoint
16:45Viseu — the granite Sé and the Igreja da Misericórdia facing each other across the Adro da Sé, and the arcaded Rua Direita running down below. An hour on foot covers the old town. Museu Grão Vasco closes at 18:00, so it's the cathedral or the museum, not both
17:45The fork of the day. Straight down the IP3 puts you in Coimbra at 19:00 with a real evening. Via the A25/A1 and the Mealhada exit you're at Luso by 18:35 — see the next two rows
18:35Only if you're running on time: Mata Nacional do Buçaco, ten minutes off the A1. In through the gate on foot to the Fonte Fria cascade and back, ~30 min — the walled 17th-century forest, 250 species, and the Palace Hotel's neo-Manueline front visible from the path. Sunset is ~19:20, so half an hour is all there is
19:20Down to Coimbra, ~30 min
19:50Check in — Studio King Deluxe, R. Lourenço de Almeida Azevedo 5, upper town near Praça da República. Confirm the arrival instructions with the host; you're getting there after dark (without the Buçaco stop this is 19:15)
20:45Dinner in the old town, downhill on foot (to book)
21:30Only if you skipped Buçaco: Coimbra fado at À Capella, in a 14th-century chapel. A different tradition from Lisbon's — sung by men, students' music, no fadista in a shawl — and this is the only night you could ever hear it
The morning is still entirely on foot. Cruise quay, station and the Casal de Loivos trailhead are all a few minutes from the Vintage House door, so the car doesn't move until 12:45. Quinta do Bomfim is dropped — it's a wine-production visit and you asked for none of those; cutting it is what buys the unhurried cruise and the DOC lunch.
DOC moves from dinner to lunch. Two things fall out of that. It's exactly en route, so the drive south costs nothing extra; and it removes the worry about whether Rui Paula's Folgosa room opens on a Wednesday evening in shoulder season. Lunch service is the reliable one. Still confirm when you book.
Wet-weather version. Skip the climb, take the Régua → Pinhão railway instead — same view, under a roof — and leave an hour earlier. Galafura is the first thing to cut if you're running late or the valley is in cloud; the viewpoint is worthless in fog.
Viseu is the new stop and it earns its place cheaply. It sits directly on the A24 line south, so it costs almost nothing in driving, and it breaks a 2h30 haul into two manageable halves. The Sé, the Misericórdia and Rua Direita are a compact hour on foot — this is a stretch-your-legs town, not a half-day.
Buçaco and the fado are the same slot — you can have one. The forest stop puts you in Coimbra at 19:50 and dinner at 20:45, which leaves no room for a 21:30 fado house after a day that started at 07:45. Going straight down the IP3 gets you in at 19:00 with the whole evening intact. My call: skip Buçaco and take the fado — thirty minutes of a forest in fading light is a consolation prize, and À Capella genuinely has no second chance. But it's a fair fight and the day is written to go either way.
Buçaco is written as a stop, not a night. The Palace Hotel room is still held — the decision is parked until late September (see the flag at the top of Flags) — but this day is built on sleeping in Coimbra, so what survives here is the gate, the Fonte Fria and a look at the facade. The full Via Sacra–Cruz Alta walk needs a bed inside the wall.
Thu8

A library at nine, a Templar convent at one, a cave at four

Coimbra → Tomar → Mira de Aire → Batalha · ends in night 5 · Hotel Lis Batalha
▶
08:15Breakfast; bags in the car. Checkout is 11:00 and firm, so clear the flat completely before you go up the hill — you're not coming back to it
08:45Walk up to the university, ~10 min uphill from the flat
09:00Biblioteca Joanina — the first slot. Timed, twenty minutes, and it sells out. Book this before anything else on the trip. Gilt, chinoiserie lacquer, and a colony of bats that eats the insects off the books at night
09:30The rest of the ticket — Capela de São Miguel, the Sala dos Capelos, the terrace over the Mondego
10:30Down through the old town — Sé Velha and the Arco de Almedina, downhill the whole way
11:15Collect the car; drive to Tomar, ~1h10
12:25Lunch in Tomar near the Praça da República — keep it light, the convent is the afternoon and it's all stairs
13:15Convento de Cristo — the Templar headquarters that became the Order of Christ when the Templars were suppressed and Portugal simply renamed them. The round Charola, built so knights could hear mass on horseback; eight cloisters stacked against each other; and the Manueline window on the chapter house — ropes, coral, seaweed and cork floats carved in stone, the single most famous piece of carving in Portugal. Allow 2h15 and don't rush the window. Last admission ~17:00
15:30Drive to Grutas de Mira de Aire, ~45 min
16:15Grutas de Mira de Aire — 110 m down into the largest show cave in Portugal, lit chambers and an underground river, and a lift back up so you only walk one direction. No small-group reservations, so allow up to half an hour of queue. Confirm the October closing time before you commit to this running order
17:15Drive to Batalha, ~25 min
17:45Check in at Hotel Lis Batalha, Largo do Mestre Afonso Domingues — the monastery square itself
18:30The monastery's west front floodlit, from the square. You can see it from the hotel, which is the whole point of sleeping here
19:45Dinner in Batalha (ask the hotel to book it — small town, quiet midweek in October)
You do not go inside the monastery today. With Tomar and the caves in the afternoon you reach Batalha at 17:45, past the ~17:30 last admission. That's fine — it opens at 09:00 and it is across the square from your bed, so Friday starts inside it. The consequence is that Friday carries four stops, which is the real cost of keeping the caves.
Tomar replaces Conímbriga, and it's a clear upgrade. Conímbriga was a Roman mosaic floor twenty minutes south of Coimbra, directly en route and worth an hour. Tomar is an hour east and costs about 40 minutes of extra driving — and it is one of the two or three best buildings in Portugal. Take the trade.
This is the tightest chain in the itinerary. Joanina 09:00 → Tomar → caves → Batalha, with a firm 11:00 checkout behind you and a cave that won't hold a slot. If the convent overruns, drop Mira de Aire, not the convent — you'll be 25 minutes from Batalha with the whole evening free instead of arriving on the hotel's doorstep at dusk.
Fri9

Two monasteries, the ocean, a walled town, and the airport

Batalha → Alcobaça → Nazaré → Óbidos → Lisbon · ends in night 6 · Lisbon
▶
08:30Breakfast; bags in the car
09:00Mosteiro da Batalha, across the square as it opens. The Founder's Chapel, the Royal Cloister's stone tracery, and the Capelas Imperfeitas — an octagonal mausoleum left open to the sky for five hundred years because the king who ordered it moved his masons to Belém. You are the first people in it
10:15Check out — the hotel allows until 12:00, so there's no pressure at this end
10:30Drive to Alcobaça, ~25 min
11:00Mosteiro de Alcobaça — Cistercian, austere, enormous, and the opposite of Batalha in every way, which is exactly why seeing both inside two hours works. The tombs of Pedro and Inês face each other across the transept, so that at the resurrection they'd see each other first. She was murdered in Coimbra, which you left yesterday. Allow an hour
12:05Drive to Nazaré, ~20 min
12:30Lunch on the seafront — a working fishing town, and the fish is the reason to be here
13:45Funicular up to Sítio, the clifftop village — Nossa Senhora da Nazaré, the Suberco balcony, the drop straight down to the beach
14:30Praia do Norte and the Forte de São Miguel Arcanjo — the lighthouse with the big-wave museum inside. Early October is the start of the season: no 25-metre day, but the canyon is already working and the swell here is unlike anywhere else in Europe
15:30Drive to Óbidos, ~45 min down the A8
16:15Óbidos — walk the walls first, while the light is still on them (unfenced in stretches, with a long drop — not for anyone uneasy with heights), then Rua Direita end to end inside the gate. Late afternoon is the right hour for the town; the coach crowd is gone and the walls go gold. The ginjinha in a chocolate cup is the local party trick — skip the ginja, keep the chocolate
17:45Drive to LIS, ~55 min
18:45Return the rental car at LIS airport — Alameda das Comunidades Portuguesas, the return area beside Terminal 1 Booked The only car of the trip, and this is where it ends. Allow 15 min to park, hand over and walk through. The booking runs to 20:30, so this is a deadline, not an appointment — arriving early is fine and costs nothing
19:20Uber to HUL · Chiado (~15 min, €12–15). Self check-in by lockbox — no host to meet, so a slow arrival costs nothing. Have the code saved offline
20:45Dinner in Chiado — Páteo — Bairro do Avillez is a minute from the door (to book)
This is the fullest day of the trip. Four stops, about 2h25 of driving, and it ends with an airport car return and letting yourself into an unfamiliar flat after dark. What makes it survivable is that nothing on it is timed — no slot, no reservation before Chiado at 20:45.
The 20:30 return time is the best thing about the car booking. The day was written against an 18:45 hand-back with no margin; you now have 1h45 of slack sitting at the end of it. Óbidos is no longer the automatic cut — a slow morning at Batalha or a long lunch in Nazaré no longer costs you the walled town. Spend the slack on the day, not on Óbidos itself: still leave around 17:45 and still return the car around 18:45, because the last hour of this day is a lockbox in an unfamiliar building and you want to reach it while you can still think. If you'd rather stretch Óbidos to sunset, leaving at 19:15 still returns the car with 15 minutes in hand — dinner just moves to about 21:30.
Alcobaça costs you almost nothing. It sits between Batalha and Nazaré, so the detour is roughly five minutes of driving against going direct — and it is the other half of Batalha. Gothic exuberance in the morning, Cistercian austerity an hour later, eighteen hours after you watched the first one floodlit from your hotel door.
Óbidos is the new stop. It's directly on the A8 into Lisbon, so like Alcobaça it barely costs driving — and late afternoon is when it's actually good. Mira de Aire has moved to Thursday, which is what made room for it.
The Friday-evening drop is confirmed by the booking itself — the return is timed at 20:30, so the desk is staffed. One thing left to check: whether your supplier's return is in the Terminal 1 car park or an off-site lot with a shuttle, which is the difference between ten minutes and thirty.

Sintra and the cape, by train

Sintra → Cabo da Roca → Cascais · ends in night 9 · Lisbon
▶
07:55Walk to Rossio station — ~8 min downhill from the flat, through Largo do Carmo and down Calçada do Carmo. No funicular needed, which is the quiet dividend of basing in Chiado

Reworked for no car, and it comes out better. Instead of driving out and doubling back, the day runs one way: rail out to Sintra, bus over the cape, rail home along the coast from Cascais. No parking, no backtracking, and the return leg is a sunset ride by the river.
Monserrate is cut to make the bus connection — it has scaffolding anyway, so it was the right thing to lose. And bus 1624 is the weak link: Scotturb runs it roughly hourly and thins out in shoulder season. Check the October timetable when you book Pena; if it doesn't work, drop Cabo da Roca and take the train straight back from Sintra.
Monday is still the correct Sintra day: Jerónimos, Belém Tower, the Coach Museum, Arte Antiga, the Azulejo Museum and the Panteão all close Mondays, while every Sintra palace stays open — and Monday is Sintra's quietest day.

Where to stay

Mid-range boutique hotels or Airbnbs throughout. Five bases, three of them single nights through the middle — that's the cost of reaching Coimbra, Tomar and the coast on the way south. All nine nights are booked — and the 7th is booked three times on purpose. Coimbra, Bussaco and Vila Julieta are all still held while the middle of the trip stays open; the plan below is written for Coimbra, which is the version that reaches Tomar. Nothing gets cancelled until late September. See the flag at the top of Flags.

Flags and recommendations

What's settled, what changed against the researched draft, and what's still open.

Holding Three rooms on the 7th, deliberately — decide in late September

You're holding all three and deciding at the start of October, while cancellation is free. That's a sound way to buy time on the one part of the trip that isn't settled, and it costs nothing if the free windows really run that late. Two things make it worth spending five minutes now.

1 · Read the actual deadline off each confirmation. Airbnb's is the most generous and the Studio King Deluxe is safe to hold longest. Booking.com auto-charges Vila Julieta the moment its window closes. Palace Hotel do Bussaco is the risk — a direct booking at a palace hotel commonly uses 14 or 21 days, which for a 7 October arrival means 23 or 16 September. If that's the case, "start of October" is already past it.

2 · The decision drives Thursday, not just the bed. Everything downstream of it stays unbookable while it's open: the Biblioteca Joanina 09:00 slot, Tomar, and the Mira de Aire timing all assume you wake in Coimbra. Those aren't urgent in shoulder season, but nothing on Thursday can be locked until this is.

The plan below is written for Coimbra, which remains the recommendation. Thursday gets long enough for Tomar — the Convento de Cristo, the Charola and the Manueline window — which is never reachable from Luso. You keep the Coimbra evening, the 09:00 Joanina slot from ten minutes below the gate, and the option of fado at À Capella. What Bussaco would buy back: the full Via Sacra–Cruz Alta forest walk and Mealhada's leitão — at the cost of Tomar and a rebuilt Thursday.

Settled Nine nights over five bases

Porto 2 · Pinhão 1 · Coimbra 1 · Batalha 1 · Lisbon 4. The Douro settled at a single night, and the freed night went forward — first to Coimbra, then split again when Batalha was booked for the 8th. That's three single nights in a row through the middle, which is more moving than the earlier versions. It buys the coast. Nazaré stops being a rushed hour wedged into a transfer day and becomes a proper afternoon, and the two great monasteries get an evening and a morning instead of a shared lunch hour.

Settled The middle of the trip, in the end

Two nights between the Douro and Lisbon, and they went to Coimbra then Batalha rather than two nights in either. That's the version that gets the university and the coast: sleeping ten minutes below the library gate is what makes the 09:00 Joanina slot possible, which in turn is what leaves Thursday long enough for Tomar — and Batalha then drops you within half an hour of Nazaré for Friday.

The cost is real — three single nights in a row, so you pack every morning from Tuesday to Friday. Against that, no leg is over 2h10 and every stop is worth the stop. Nazaré finally gets a real afternoon rather than the 15:15–17:30 slot it had when it was wedged into a transfer day.

Gained Saturday the 10th is now a free Lisbon day

Previously it was a Nazaré→Óbidos→Lisbon transfer with a squeezed evening. Now it's a full unhurried day: Feira da Ladra (Saturdays only), the Panteão dome, the castle, Alfama and Baixa. Nothing on it is timed, so it doubles as the buffer and the rain day.

Cost Friday the 9th is the fullest day of the trip

Four stops and about 2h25 of driving: the Batalha monastery at 09:00, Alcobaça, Nazaré, Óbidos, then the airport car return and Chiado after dark. It holds because nothing on it is timed and the first stop is across the square from where you wake up — but it has no slack.

Óbidos is the cut if the day slips. It's 55 minutes from Lisbon on the A8 and Saturday the 10th is deliberately empty, so it survives as a day trip. Alcobaça is not the cut — it's five minutes of detour and it's the other half of Batalha.

Lost Peneda-Gerês and the Sistelo hike are gone

The researched draft had a night at Arcos de Valdevez and the PR24 Socalcos de Sistelo trail — both listed under partner feedback as included. The night structure has no room for them; the detour is 2h45 north of the Porto→Douro line. Paiva on the 6th is the substitute, and I think it's a fair one, but it is a substitution and not a reshuffle.

Booked Pinhão — The Vintage House, night 3

This settles the Pinhão-versus-Régua question in Pinhão's favour, and Wednesday is the proof: The cruise quay, the station panels and the Casal de Loivos trailhead are all on foot from the door, so the car doesn't move at all between arriving Tuesday evening and leaving for Folgosa at 12:45 on Wednesday. It also guarantees the thing that kept eliminating the small quintas — a kitchen that serves dinner every night in October — which matters on Tuesday, when you arrive off Paiva wanting to eat where you sleep. And it hands back the moderate hike lost with Sistelo: the climb to Casal de Loivos.

What you give up is seclusion. Pinhão is the valley's tourist hub — boats, coaches, the station — so you wake in a village rather than on a terraced hillside. Ask explicitly for a refurbished, river-facing room; the refurbishment reached the property unevenly and the reviews split on exactly that line.

Settled Batalha on the 8th is the best structural decision of the trip

It doesn't look like much — a one-night stop in a town of six thousand people — but it fixes the two things that were still wrong with the plan.

It fixes Friday. The old Friday ran Coimbra → Mira de Aire → Batalha → Nazaré → Lisbon: four stops, 2h45 of driving, on the same day you return the car and let yourself into a strange flat at 19:20. It's now three short hops and about 2h10, with the monastery already behind you.

It gets you the ocean. That was the one thing the itinerary had none of. Nazaré goes from a squeezed hour to two full hours — lunch on the seafront, the funicular up to Sítio, and Praia do Norte with the season just starting.

And it pairs the monasteries properly. Batalha floodlit on Thursday evening from your own doorstep, Alcobaça on Friday morning — Gothic exuberance against Cistercian austerity, eighteen hours apart. Alcobaça also closes a loop: the tombs of Pedro and Inês face each other across the transept, and Inês was murdered in Coimbra, which you'll have left the day before.

What it costs. A third consecutive single night, and with it the Buçaco forest walk and leitão at Mealhada — both of which needed two nights in the area. What went into the Thursday afternoon they vacated is Tomar, an hour east, and it is worth more than either.

Budget The splurge is banked, and that's now final

The working split was Douro €400–550 × 2 · Lisbon €180–240 × 4 · Coimbra €110–160 × 1. What actually got booked: Lisbon at ~€130 a night (₪2,080 for four, against a €720–960 budget), one mid-priced Douro night instead of two splurge nights, and the middle two nights in a Coimbra studio and a small Batalha hotel rather than a country estate.

Every stop came in under — call it €700–1,000 of accommodation budget unspent. The splurge got designed out of the trip one sensible decision at a time, and Bussaco is the last place it could land — which is the one honest argument for keeping that room held. If the money is going to go anywhere, it goes there. Let it go and the question is closed: no night left can absorb it.

That's a defensible outcome, not a failure — you bought Tomar and a Coimbra evening with it. If you want the money back in the trip, the levers left are meals (Friday night in Chiado is unbooked and Belcanto is four minutes from the door) or a room upgrade at NOLIA or the Vintage House, both of which you're already paying to be in.

Do now Email NOLIA about the 09:00 landing

You're booked from the 4th, but Portuguese check-in is 15:00 and the day is scheduled from 11:45 — so the room has to be ready at 10:30 or the morning falls apart. Three ways out, cheapest first: ask for a free early check-in (small boutique hotels often say yes if you tell them the flight number weeks ahead); pay a half-day early-arrival fee; or add the night of the 3rd, which guarantees it. Whichever you pick, get it in writing. Worst case, agree bag drop plus access to a bathroom and shift the shower to the 17:30 gap.

Settled Lisbon is car-free

One rental for the whole trip, dropped at LIS on the way in from Nazaré on the 9th. Two consequences, both fine: Sintra becomes a rail-and-bus day — and a better one, since it now runs one way out to the cape and home along the coast from Cascais instead of doubling back — and Arrábida is gone, because Portinho is genuinely hard to reach without driving. Do now Nobody holds your bags on the 13th

You check out around 09:30 and fly at 22:15 — nine hours with luggage. Self check-in by lockbox is the right call for Friday night, but it cuts the other way on Tuesday: there is no host on site to leave bags with, so this one has no fallback and has to be booked. Worth asking the host anyway in case the building has a luggage room — but book the locker regardless.

Watch Nazaré is now weather-dependent in a way it wasn't

The big-wave season at Praia do Norte starts around now, and you have one two-hour window on Friday afternoon instead of an evening plus a morning. If a big swell is forecast for the 9th it's worth stretching the stop; if it's flat, leave early and get to Lisbon in daylight.
